Good evening everyone! Tonight in my free time, I finally sat down to clean up my chaotic browser bookmarks and build a proper **curated reading list**.

Not gonna lie, finding genuinely good blogs these days is harder than it used to be. Half the search results are corporate vendor blogs trying to sell you a SaaS subscription, auto-generated AI slop, or old domains that expired and turned into sketchy betting sites.

Since I need to record a lot of stuff so I can remember it properly, I filtered out the noise and locked in **30 high-signal blogs and repositories** across 10 categories that I actually use for work and personal growth.

**Extra Example:** Think of your daily reading feed like your diet. Scrolling random social feeds and vendor marketing is like eating gorengan every night—easy to consume, zero nutrition, and you feel sluggish afterward. Curating a tight list of independent writers is like meal-prepping clean food on Sunday so you always have something solid ready during a busy week.

## 1. Self Improvement (Discipline)

Staying consistent after a long day of staring at queries and Jakarta traffic requires systems, not pure willpower. These three focus on practical habit mechanics and daily execution without toxic hustle-culture preaching.

- **Raptitude (David Cain)** — [raptitude.com](https://www.raptitude.com/) — Deep, practical essays on habit friction, attention control, and running small personal discipline experiments.
- **Time Management Ninja (Craig Jarrow)** — [timemanagementninja.com](https://timemanagementninja.com/) — Short, tactical posts focused on ruthless prioritization, daily execution, and cutting time-wasting habits.
- **James Clear’s Articles** — [jamesclear.com/articles](https://jamesclear.com/articles) — Bestselling author of *Atomic Habits*, sharing objective, systems-first playbooks on habit mechanics and focus.

## 2. Self Improvement (Psychology)

I enjoy psychology most when it gives concrete, usable tips rather than drowning you in 40-page academic study summaries. These three break down human behavior into clean, scannable takeaways.

- **Barking Up The Wrong Tree (Eric Barker)** — [bakadesuyo.com](https://bakadesuyo.com/) — Research-backed, witty "how-to" behavioral psychology on resilience, motivation, and better decisions.
- **Nick Wignall** — [nickwignall.com](https://nickwignall.com/) — Granular, simple mental habits with clear numbered-tip structures written by a clinical psychologist.
- **The Positivity Blog (Henrik Edberg)** — [positivityblog.com](https://www.positivityblog.com/) — Simple, scannable numbered-tip articles on mindset, focus, and everyday psychology.

## 3. Self Improvement (General)

For broader thinking—how to learn complex skills faster, make clearer decisions, and organize notes—these three are my go-to reads.

- **Scott H. Young** — [scotthyoung.com/blog](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/) — Evidence-backed writing on **ultralearning**, rapid cognitive skill acquisition, and structured self-education.
- **Farnam Street (Shane Parrish)** — [fs.blog](https://fs.blog/) — Author of *Clear Thinking*, breaking down mental models and decision-making frameworks without bookish fluff.
- **Ness Labs (Anne-Laure Le Cunff)** — [nesslabs.com/blog](https://nesslabs.com/blog) — Thematic, experiment-driven frameworks for learning, metacognition, and note-taking.

## 4. Data Analytics (Python)

In day-to-day banking analytics, you need two things in Python: rock-solid command of core libraries (`numpy`, `pandas`, `scikit-learn`, `statsmodels`) and the ability to fix memory or speed bottlenecks when datasets grow.

- **Python Speed (Itamar Turner-Trauring)** — [pythonspeed.com](https://pythonspeed.com/) — Deep technical guides on Pandas/Polars memory profiling, CPU bottlenecks, and speeding up Python pipelines.
- **Calmcode** — [calmcode.io/blog](https://calmcode.io/blog) — Clean microlearning posts on modern Python data libraries, testing, and lightweight tooling.
- **Python Data Science Handbook (Jake VanderPlas)** — [jakevdp.github.io/PythonDataScienceHandbook](https://jakevdp.github.io/PythonDataScienceHandbook/) — Clean, free, zero-ad open-source reference mastering `NumPy`, `Pandas`, `Matplotlib`, and `Scikit-Learn`.

> **Note:** For statistical modeling, regression diagnostics, and time-series notebooks in Python, I pair Jake VanderPlas's handbook directly with the official [statsmodels Examples Gallery](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/examples/index.html).

## 5. Data Analytics (SQL)

I write SQL almost every single day, so I appreciate sites with a clean, modern UI that tackle real-world analytical problems instead of legacy early-2000s web layouts.

- **Select Star SQL (Zi Chong Kao)** — [selectstarsql.com](https://selectstarsql.com/) — Crisp, modern, interactive walkthroughs tackling real-world analytical SQL problems.
- **Haki Benita** — [hakibenita.com](https://hakibenita.com/) — Clean, modern technical deep dives solving everyday PostgreSQL, analytical SQL, and query tuning bottlenecks.
- **Mode SQL Tutorial** — [mode.com/sql-tutorial](https://mode.com/sql-tutorial/) — Clean, structured progression from basic `GROUP BY` and `JOIN`s into window functions, CTEs, subqueries, and pivoting.

## 6. Data Analytics (Excel)

Even if you live in Python and SQL, Excel is still the universal language of business stakeholders. Knowing modern dynamic arrays and Power Query saves massive amounts of manual cleanup.

- **Exceljet** — [exceljet.net/blog](https://exceljet.net/blog) — Concise, formula-first breakdowns showing exact syntax and nested formula edge cases.
- **XelPlus (Leila Gharani)** — [xelplus.com/tutorials](https://www.xelplus.com/tutorials/) — Clear written tutorials on dynamic arrays (`LET`, `LAMBDA`, `FILTER`, `XLOOKUP`) and Power Query.
- **My Online Training Hub (Mynda Treacy)** — [myonlinetraininghub.com/blog](https://www.myonlinetraininghub.com/blog) — Clean, modern layout with step-by-step written guides on dynamic arrays, `LAMBDA`, and Power Query.

## 7. Dashboard (Tableau)

When building executive dashboards, you want clean UI templates, smart layout grids, and clever level-of-detail calculations—not video-only feeds where you have to scrub through a 20-minute clip for one formula.

- **The Flerlage Twins** — [flerlagetwins.com](https://www.flerlagetwins.com/) — Custom charts, LOD expressions, parameter actions, and precision layout design.
- **Data + Science (Jeffrey Shaffer)** — [dataplusscience.com](https://www.dataplusscience.com/) — Tableau performance optimization, custom map layers, and dataviz best practices.
- **Kevin Wee’s Blog of Visualization** — [kevinwee.com/tag/tableau](https://kevinwee.com/tag/tableau/) — Actively updated blog packed with free downloadable Figma layout templates, custom `.tps` color palettes, and clean UI/UX dashboard guides.

## 8. Data Visualization & Storytelling

A technically accurate chart is useless if stakeholders cannot read it in five seconds. These three help sharpen visual hierarchy, color restraint, and storytelling.

- **Storytelling with Data (Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic)** — [storytellingwithdata.com/blog](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog) — Practical before-and-after chart makeovers focusing on visual hierarchy, color restraint, and executive storytelling.
- **Visual Cinnamon (Nadieh Bremer)** — [visualcinnamon.com/blog](https://www.visualcinnamon.com/blog/) — Artistic inspiration and deep breakdowns of custom color palettes and visual encoding.
- **Makeover Monday** — [makeovermonday.co.uk](https://www.makeovermonday.co.uk/) — Community-driven Tableau storytelling showcase with downloadable `.twbx` templates, color/layout inspiration, and before-and-after visual comparisons.

## 9. Niche FOSS & GitHub Open-Source Discovery

I love finding lightweight, open-source CLI and terminal tools that do one job well without bloated Electron wrappers. These three make discovering cool GitHub repositories effortless.

- **Terminal Trove** — [terminaltrove.com](https://terminaltrove.com/) — Niche, high-utility command-line (CLI) and terminal UI (TUI) open-source tools.
- **OpenCurious — Open-Source GitHub Spotlights** — [opencurious.com/explore-open-source](https://www.opencurious.com/explore-open-source) — Clean, categorized discovery feed highlighting fast-rising GitHub CLI, desktop, and developer projects.
- **LibHunt — Trending Open-Source & GitHub Comparisons** — [libhunt.com](https://www.libhunt.com/) — Categorized GitHub repository discovery with clear context, feature breakdowns, and side-by-side open-source alternatives.

## 10. Prompting & Prompt Libraries

Honestly, most "prompt engineering" sites are just paid course funnels or heavy ML theory papers. These three focus purely on practical prompt structures, structured outputs, and copy-pasteable templates for Claude and Gemini.

- **Eugene Yan** — [eugeneyan.com](https://eugeneyan.com/) — Clean, minimalist engineering essays on practical prompting fundamentals, structured I/O contracts, and prompt refactoring.
- **Prompting Guide (`dair.ai`)** — [promptingguide.ai](https://www.promptingguide.ai/) — Well-designed visual reference covering practical prompting patterns (few-shot, structured outputs, decomposition) without dense academic math.
- **Awesome Claude Prompts & Google `gemini-cookbook`** — [github.com/langgptai/awesome-claude-prompts](https://github.com/langgptai/awesome-claude-prompts) & [github.com/google-gemini/cookbook](https://github.com/google-gemini/cookbook) — Open-source GitHub repositories with copy-pasteable system prompts, XML artifact builders, and structured-output recipes.

## Conclusion: Pick Three and Put Them in an RSS Reader

Having 30 great blogs is useless if you try to read all of them every single day. Pick **one self-improvement blog**, **one data analytics blog**, and **one discovery feed** (like Terminal Trove or LibHunt) to check over coffee this week, and drop the rest into an RSS reader so you never rely on noisy social algorithms again.
