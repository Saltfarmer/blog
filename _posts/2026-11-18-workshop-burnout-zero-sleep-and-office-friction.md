Hello again, everyone. I'll try to keep this one short. Recently, I had to spend two days at a workshop as an operator. Once again, the problem started because I wasn't expecting to be an actual participant—mostly because I wasn't assigned to any group. On top of that, I still had my own urgent tasks to finish, which were also important since they're tied to a Strategic Initiative. Let me try to tell the story chronologically.

## Pre-Workshop

As an operator, my main job was to make several video bumpers for different speakers and design a "KV" (key visual, as they called it). I was totally fine with that. I already had the resources, templates, and references from previous events, so I ended up making around five bumpers and a few KV variations. Looking back, maybe this is where my first mistake happened (or maybe not).

The workshop was scheduled for Monday and Tuesday, but the committee decided to hold a *gladi bersih* (dress rehearsal) on Sunday. On an effin' Sunday afternoon. I forgot to make up an excuse that I couldn't come, so I ended up going anyway. Honestly, they didn't even need me that much there. Still, I felt kinda bad—mostly because I didn't speak up and say I didn't want to spend my Sunday working. It felt like too much to ask from an operator when it wasn't even my main job.

In the end, skipping out early or showing reluctance just left a bad impression. The committee said, "It's fine, it's alright," though we all know it really wasn't.

## Day 1 Workshop

For the first day, the committee asked us to be ready at 6 AM. To be fair, it was kinda my fault for not going to bed early and telling my girlfriend I needed to rest. I only got five hours of sleep, woke up dizzy, and couldn't focus at all. I also had to skip my daily morning exercise, which ruined a good habit I've been trying to maintain (sorry, Bubu).

I arrived at 6 AM without breakfast and asked my friend what I needed to prepare. He said nothing much—just handle the bumpers and display the screen backgrounds. Good enough for me. So I focused on doing my operator duties while squeezing in my own urgent work during the group discussion sessions, since I wasn't assigned to any group.

Then, out of nowhere, my DH (Department Head) came over to my table.

> **DH:** "Candra, what are you doing?"  
>
> **Me:** "Writing a report, Teh. I also have a request from my friend that I need to fix."  
>
> **DH:** "Have you been assigned to any group?"  
>
> **Me:** "No, Teh, I haven't been assigned to any group."  
>
> **DH:** "Can you join Group X? I think they need help with your data insights on Treasury and Trade Finance."  
>
> **Me:** "What kind of help, Teh? I don't think they need me since they're way more expert on that than I am. If they need anything, they'll ask me directly anyway."  
>
> **DH:** "I don't know. Instead of <u>not doing anything here</u>, you could join their group and at least listen to them."  
>
> **Me:** *(What the efff, "not doing anything"?)* "Alright, Teh, I'll ask them later."

So I texted one of the group members to ask if they needed a hand. He said not right now, and that he'd let me know if they did. Because my mood was already ruined, I awkwardly tried to look useful anyway. They eventually asked me to look up a business process. I searched for it, couldn't find it, and only ended up finding the rules for defining "Bad Data."

By 5:30 PM, I asked my friend, "When is this supposed to end? It's already late, why are they still working? Can I go home?" He warned me it was too risky to leave because everyone else was still hyped up and working. I didn't get it—don't we still have tomorrow? By 6 PM, I felt completely unproductive and just wanted to go home and see my girlfriend.

> **DH:** "Candra, where are you going?"  
>
> **Me:** *(WTF)* "I'm going home, Teh."  
>
> **DH:** "Hmmmm."  
>
> **Me:** *(Eff it, I'm still leaving.)*

I went home hoping to vent to my girlfriend about my day. Unfortunately, she was already asleep. She woke up briefly and said a few things that kinda broke my sensitive heart, though it's alright—she was probably insanely tired too. We had stayed up until midnight on both Friday and Sunday, so both of us were running on fumes.

## Day 2 Workshop

On Day 2, I arrived a little late. There was even less for me to do—it was insanely boring, and I was still so effin' tired and sleepy. Since I wasn't in the mood to sit around "doing nothing," I stepped out of the room to get some actual work done and chatted with the support team, which was actually pretty fun.

Later, I heard people were mentioning that I left early the day before. At that point, I just thought, *alright, eff it, I just need sleep.* I'll just pitch in and help cover the closing wrap-up with the other staff later. Eventually, the day ended, and I finally got to unwind with my girlfriend—doing some shopping and eating mutton soup and mutton fried rice.

## After the Workshop (Today)

Finally back at the office. Things are much more chill today because I managed to finish some of my tasks over the past two days, so I can focus on improvements and maintenance. The committee kept joking around about "9 hours of doing nothing." I laughed at first, though it kinda sounded like sarcasm—or maybe my mood just hasn't fully recovered yet.

In the end, I vented to my friend about how I felt dealing with my DH: too many random demands and a total lack of appreciation. It's so freakin' annoying. Still, I'm writing this down on the blog because I probably need to reflect on my own behavior too. Beefing with another DH isn't really a coincidence, so maybe there are things I need to handle better myself.

That's all for today, folks! Thanks for reading. I'll catch you in the next post.
